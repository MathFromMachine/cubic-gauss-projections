1. FIELD, CHARACTERS, AND NORMALIZATION

Let O=Z[omega], where omega=exp(2*pi*i/3). Write N(a)=|O/a| for a
nonzero integral ideal a. Let lambda=omega-1 and Lambda=1+2*omega.
The two elements are associates; keeping Lambda explicit fixes the
additive-character convention. Use the canonical primary generator
n_a=primaryGenerator(a) from the upstream source. Its exceptional value
zero is retained exactly as in that definition.

For every prime ideal p away from lambda, let chi_{6,p} be the source's
canonical local sextic character, extended by zero on the nonunits of
O/p. The restriction on residue characteristic 2 applies only to the
supported moving ideal I in section 2; it is not a restriction on the
squarefree ideals occurring in gamma. In particular, those ideals may
contain the prime ideal (2). Powers below mean powers of zero-extended
multiplicative characters. Exponent zero is the principal character with
value zero at zero, not the constant-one function. For a squarefree ideal
a with n_a nonzero, set chi_a=product_{p|a} chi_{6,p}^2, the corresponding
cubic character.

Define the normalized source Gauss sum by the exact trace convention

    g_2(a) = (Na)^(-1/2) sum_{x mod a} chi_a(x)
                         exp(2*pi*i*Tr_{Q(omega)/Q}(x/(Lambda*n_a))).

Here the quotient is identified through (n_a)=a. The equality to the
Lean convention is quotientTrace_mk together with
cuspFrequency(h)=embedding(h)/(3*Lambda). This is the same character used
by gaussTwo; the normalization denominator is |n_a|=sqrt(Na).
Set theta_{-1}(n)=conj(n/|n|) and

    gamma(a) = theta_{-1}(n_a)*g_2(a)

when a is squarefree and n_a is nonzero, and gamma(a)=0 otherwise.
Thus gamma is precisely squarefreeGaussCoefficient, including its
squarefree and primary-support masks. |gamma(a)|<=1.

For comparison only, if G_paper(a) uses exp(2*pi*i*Tr(x/n_a)) instead,
change variables x=Lambda*y to obtain

    g_2(a)=chi_a(Lambda)*G_paper(a),
    gamma(a)=theta_{-1}(n_a)*chi_a(Lambda)*G_paper(a).

There is no conjugation of G_paper in this formula. Omitting the fixed
Lambda twist would change the mathematical statement. The theorem below
uses gamma as defined above and does not depend on translating conventions.

2. PRECISE THEOREM

Fix a smooth W:R->C and real lo,hi with lo>0 and supp(W) contained in
[lo,hi]. There is C_W>=0, chosen before all the following data.

Let I be a nonzero integral ideal, with every prime factor avoiding
lambda and having residue characteristic different from 2. This is
exactly CanonicalQuadraticSieve.Supported(I). Let Q be any nonzero
integral ideal. Let phi:O->C be multiplicative, with phi(1)=1,
|phi(n)|<=1 for every n, and phi factoring through O/Q.

Let F be the set of prime ideals p dividing I with Q not contained in p.
Equivalently, F consists of prime factors of I outside the fixed modulus.
For p in F put q_p=Np and j_p=v_p(I) mod 6, and define

    Psi(n) = phi(n) product_{p in F} chi_{6,p}(n mod p)^(j_p).
    P_Psi,W(L) = sum_a gamma(a)*Psi(n_a)*W(Na/L),    L>0.

The character powers in this display retain the zero-extension masks
specified in section 1. The sum is over all integral ideals; only finitely
many terms survive the support of W and gamma. Define

    d_0(q) = 1-q^(-1)+q^(1/2),
    d_4(q) = 2*q^(1/2)+q^(-1),
    d_j(q) = q                  for j=1,2,3,5,
    D(I,Q) = product_{p in F} d_{j_p}(q_p).

Then, for every L>0,

    |P_Psi,W(L)| <= C_W sqrt(L) (NQ)^(3/4) sqrt(D(I,Q)).             (1)

The empty product is one. There is no restriction on the number of
moving primes or their mixture of exponents. C_W is independent of
I,Q,phi,L. The fixed modulus price (NQ)^(3/4) has not been absorbed into
an allegedly uniform constant.

Exact Lean statement:
QRHExperimental.CompositeProjectionCosts.rawProjection_composite_prime_product
in lean/CompositeProjectionCosts.lean. The companion theorem
sourceCost_eq_prime_product proves the displayed table uses actual ideal
norms. This is a fixed-W theorem; profile-uniform applications require
additional uniform seminorm estimates.

3. COMPLETION AND THE EXACT INVERSE

Write V(y)=sqrt(y)*W(y) and use the upstream multiplicative cube weight

    c_Psi(b) = conj(n_b/|n_b|)^3 * Psi(n_b)^3 / Nb,

with the source's zero convention when n_b=0. It satisfies
|c_Psi(b)|<=1/Nb. Define the actual completed source

    T_Psi,W(X) = sum_{a,b} [gamma(a)*Psi(n_a)/sqrt(Na)] * c_Psi(b)
                         * V(Na*(Nb)^3/X).

This is completedT. The factors sqrt(Na), V and c_Psi must be kept in
this exact arrangement; changing them alters the cube-inversion weight.
Upstream's finite-support ideal Mobius convolution gives

    P_Psi,W(L)/sqrt(L)
      = sum_h mu(h)*c_Psi(h)*T_Psi,W(L/(Nh)^3).                    (2)

It holds for every positive L and compactly supported W. We will use
only |mu(h)|<=1 and |c_Psi(h)|<=1/Nh, not cancellation of this sum.
The norm-one Gauss bound and linear ideal counting give, uniformly in
bounded multiplicative Psi,

    |P_Psi,W(L)| <= C_W L,
    |T_Psi,W(X)| <= C_W sqrt(X).                                  (3)

The relevant exact source is GaussSum/CompletedExpansion.lean,
completed_cube_inverse. Included files TrivialProjection.lean,
TrivialCompleted.lean, UniformTrivial.lean and CubeInverseAssembly.lean
supply the norm bounds and the explicit inversion/balancing assembly.

4. SPARSE RADIAL MASS AND THE KERNEL

The three actual cusp coefficient systems have nonzero frequencies
indexed by a unit u, an integer m>=0, a squarefree eligible ideal a and
an eligible ideal b. Apart from fixed cusp factors of bounded modulus,
the radial length and an absolute coefficient majorant are

    rho = 3^m*(Na)*(Nb)^3,
    w   = 3^(-m/3)*(Na)^(-1/2)*(Nb)^(-1).

These formulas are the source's squarefree-times-cube support theorem;
no random-phase or independent-coefficient assumption is introduced.
Linear ideal counting implies
sum_{Na<=U}(Na)^(-1/2)<=C sqrt(U). Consequently

    sum_{rho<=T} w <= C sqrt(T)

because summing first over a leaves
sqrt(T)*3^(-5m/6)*(Nb)^(-5/2), and both remaining sums converge.
The finite unit sectors and three cusps affect only C.

The same argument keeps prescribed divisors simultaneously. For nonzero
ideals A,B, multiplication of ideals is injective and norms multiply, so

    sum_{rho<=T, A|a, B|b} w
      <= C sqrt(T)*(NA)^(-1)*(NB)^(-5/2).                          (4)

The constant is the SAME for every A,B. One must not apply a separate
one-prime estimate and then raise an unrecorded constant to the number
of primes. DivisorIdealMass.lean and DivisorRadialMass.lean prove (4);
CompositeModelKernel.lean performs the finite branch expansion with
one global constant.

The existing smooth reflection kernel K_W satisfies
|K_W(t)|<=A_W for 0<t<=1 and |K_W(t)|<=A_W/t for t>=1.
If nonnegative mass has cumulative bound C sqrt(T), split at rho=1/v.
The lower range contributes at most C*A_W*v^(-1/2). On upper bands
4^k/v<rho<=4^(k+1)/v, divide the band mass by its lower radial endpoint;
the resulting geometric series is at most 4*C*A_W*v^(-1/2). Therefore

    sum |b_rho*K_W(v*rho)| <= 5*C*A_W*v^(-1/2).                   (5)

This also proves absolute summability. WeightedKernelBound.lean and
EndpointProjection.lean implement this endpoint argument, including
the smooth-kernel estimates drawn from the upstream Mellin machinery.

5. EXACT LOCAL BRACKETS AND SIMULTANEOUS PRIME COSTS

Fix any active subset S of the moving primes. The source's exact
controlled reflection formula contains a product of local brackets,
not an arbitrary finite Fourier transform. For q=Np and exponent j,
their bounds are

    j=0:       |B_{p,0}(x)| <= q^(-1/2),
    j=4:       |B_{p,4}(x)| <= q^(-1/2)+q^(1/2)*1_{p|x},
    j=1,2,3,5: |B_{p,j}(x)| <= 1.

For every nonzero supported frequency in each of the three cusps, the
phase numerator x is a unit times a power of lambda times n_a*n_b^3.
As every moving prime is good, p|x holds exactly when p|a or p|b.
Use 1_{p|x}<=1_{p|a}+1_{p|b}. Expand the finite product over p in S.
For each branch choice, group its a-divisors into A and its b-divisors
into B. Distinct moving primes are coprime. Applying (4) once, then
summing the branch choices, gives the local reflected factors

    k_0(q) = q^(-1/2),
    k_4(q) = q^(-1/2)+q^(1/2)*q^(-1)+q^(1/2)*q^(-5/2)
           = 2*q^(-1/2)+q^(-2),
    k_j(q) = 1                     for j=1,2,3,5.                 (6)

All source phases outside the brackets have norm at most one or fixed
absolute norm. Combining (5) with the exact reflection identity gives
ONE constant C_W, uniform over S, every prime family, and source geometry.
The precise stratum bound is

    |F_S(X)| <= C_W product_{p in S} k_{j_p}(q_p)
         * [X/(27*s_cusp^2*N(c0*product_{p in S} pi_p)^2)]^(-1/2), (7)

where pi_p is the source's primary generator of p. The geometry has
c0 dividing c, with (c)=(9)*Q. The three cusp scales satisfy the checked
uniform estimate

    bracketed factor in (7) <= 16*N(c)*product_{p in S} q_p/sqrt(X).

Thus active local cost is a_j(q)=q*k_j(q). No dependence of C_W on the
number of moving primes has been introduced.

The source lemmas are GaussSum/ReflectionBrackets.lean and
Eisenstein/FixedConductor.lean; the actual frequency/divisibility adapter
is in PhaseNumeratorSupport.lean. CompositeBracketDomination.lean and
CompositeControlledReflection.lean apply the exact source formula.

6. ALL ACTIVE AND INACTIVE STRATA, THEN THE FIXED MODULUS

Upstream fixedFree_completedT_eq_actualControlledSource is an exact
identity. It expresses T as thetaDerivativeScalar^(-1) times the fixed
Fourier sum and an inner sum over ALL active subsets S. A prime outside
S contributes

    b_j(q) = 1-1/q if j=0, and 0 otherwise.

This coefficient is real and nonnegative. It follows directly from the
exact zero-frequency Fourier coefficient of the zero-extended character.
For j=0 it cannot be deleted. Using (7) and the same C_W for every S,

the sum of absolute stratum bounds contains exactly

    sum_{S subset F} product_{p notin S} b_{j_p}(q_p)
                         * product_{p in S} a_{j_p}(q_p)
      = product_{p in F} (a_{j_p}(q_p)+b_{j_p}(q_p)) = D(I,Q).     (8)

The equality is the finite product expansion. In particular, no extra
factor for the number of subsets is needed. The local factor 2 in d_4
is retained, not hidden in C_W.

The normalized fixed Fourier coefficients satisfy Parseval and hence
sum_h |fixedThetaRowCoeff(h)|<=sqrt(N(c)), since |phi|<=1.
Combine this with the N(c) factor in (7) and (8). Since N(c)=81*NQ,
the fixed factor 81 and theta scalar are absorbed into C_W, giving

    |T_Psi,W(X)| <= C_W (NQ)^(3/2) D(I,Q) X^(-1/2).              (9)

All fixed bad-prime masks, unit sectors, primary choices, and coprimality
conditions are those of the exact source identity. ActiveStratumBound
proves (8), FiniteBaseBounds proves the geometry/Parseval bounds, and
CompositeProjection proves (9) on the actual source.

7. BALANCING THE CUBE INVERSE

Put R=(NQ)^(3/2)*D(I,Q)>0. If L<=R, the first bound in (3) already gives
C_W L<=C_W sqrt(L)*sqrt(R). Otherwise set U=(L/R)^(1/3)>=1 in (2).
For Nh<=U use (9); for Nh>U use the second bound in (3). The absolute
value of the normalized inverse is at most

    C_W [R*L^(-1/2) sum_{Nh<=U}(Nh)^(1/2)
                          +sqrt(L) sum_{Nh>U}(Nh)^(-5/2)].

Linear ideal counting, with partial summation or dyadic bands, gives
sum_{Nh<=U}(Nh)^(1/2)<=128*U^(3/2) and
sum_{Nh>U}(Nh)^(-5/2)<=512*U^(-3/2).
Both terms are O_W(sqrt(R)) at this U. Multiply (2) by sqrt(L) to obtain
(1). This proves the theorem for every L>0. IdealPowerSums.lean and
CubeInverseAssembly.lean verify these inequalities and the exact balance.

8. COPRIMALITY MASKS BY EXACT EXTRACTION

In addition to the data of section 2, let M be supported and admissible:
M is squarefree and its canonical primary generator is nonzero. Suppose
M is coprime to both I and Q. Define

    mask_M(n) = 1 if (M,(n))=1, and 0 otherwise,
    E(M) = product_{p|M}[1+sqrt(2(Np)^(-1/2)+(Np)^(-2))].

There is a single C_W, chosen before I,M,Q,phi,L, such that

    |P_{Psi*mask_M,W}(L)|
      <= C_W sqrt(L) (NQ)^(3/4) sqrt(D(I,Q)) E(M).                 (10)

For every epsilon>0, E(M)<=C_epsilon (NM)^epsilon uniformly in M.
Thus (10) also holds with E(M) replaced by (NM)^epsilon, and the one
constant replaced by C_{W,epsilon}. The fixed profile W and epsilon
are chosen before that constant; all ideals and characters come after it.

Here is the complete reduction. For admissible squarefree D the exact
Gauss CRT identity, including shared-factor zeros, is

    gamma(DJ)=gamma(D) gamma(J) fourthTwist_D(n_J).

The character fourthTwist_D is the square of the cubic ideal character,
equivalently the canonical sextic character at exponent four. Its
orientation is fixed by the upstream multiplicativity theorem; no Gauss
conjugation is introduced. Multiplicativity of Psi and ideal norms then
identifies the part of P_Psi,W(L) supported on D-divisible ideals as

    columnWeight(Psi,D) P_{Psi*fourthTwist_D,W}(L/ND).

Terms sharing a factor between D and J vanish on both sides. Inadmissible
primary-generator and nonsquarefree terms retain their original zeros.
Finite ideal Mobius inclusion-exclusion consequently gives the exact
identity, even without coprimality assumptions between Psi and M,

    P_{Psi*mask_M,W}(L)
      = sum_{D|M} mu(D) columnWeight(Psi,D)
                          P_{Psi*fourthTwist_D,W}(L/ND).           (11)

The coefficient outside the shifted projection has absolute value at
most one. Compact support justifies the finite rearrangements.
MaskExtraction.lean verifies (11) for the actual source coefficient.

Because D divides M, its prime support is disjoint from I and Q. The
exact source row identity and its local cost factorization are

    freePrimeRow(I,Q)*fourthTwist_D = freePrimeRow(I D^4,Q),
    D(I D^4,Q) = D(I,Q) product_{p|D}[2sqrt(Np)+(Np)^(-1)].

MaskSourceAdapter.lean proves both. The first identity only requires
coprimality of D and Q; disjointness from I is used in the second.
Apply (1) to each shifted projection in (11). Its constant is chosen
before D, so it appears only once outside the divisor sum. The remaining
factor equals

    sum_{D|M} sqrt(product_{p|D}[2sqrt(Np)+(Np)^(-1)])/sqrt(ND)
      = sum_{D|M} product_{p|D}sqrt(2(Np)^(-1/2)+(Np)^(-2))
      = product_{p|M}[1+sqrt(2(Np)^(-1/2)+(Np)^(-2))].

The first equality uses exact norm factorization for squarefree ideals;
the second is the finite subset Euler identity. This proves (10).
For q>=1 every factor is <=4, so E(M)<=4^omega(M)=(2^omega(M))^2.
The upstream uniform prime-support subset bound at epsilon/2, squared,
gives E(M)<=C_epsilon (NM)^epsilon. The finite identity, norm conversion,
and subpower inequality are checked in MaskEulerProduct.lean. The full
actual corollaries are checked in MaskedCompositeProjection.lean.

The displayed E(M) formula assumes a separate disjoint mask. Overlap is
handled by explicit identities, not by pretending the same factorization
holds unchanged: an admissible mask D dividing Q factors modulo Q and
can be absorbed into phi without changing the modulus or its norm bound;
a mask D dividing I and coprime to Q is already present in freePrimeRow
and is redundant. Products of admissible masks split multiplicatively.
MaskOverlap.lean checks these identities. They allow a prime partition
into fixed-base, existing-moving, and genuinely new mask support; the
remaining new support is the M in (10).

For a single large prime the new factor is 1+O(q^(-1/4)). It need not be
smaller than sqrt(d_0(q)) at every small prime. Neither upper bound is
claimed optimal. In the pure cubic angular specialization this extraction
is an elementary addition to the adjacent Patterson angular input;
its inclusion here is not a claim of analytic novelty.

CubicGaussProjections.coprimality_mask_projection, and
CubicGaussProjections.coprimality_mask_subpower.

9. PROVENANCE, SCOPE, AND VERIFICATION

The field, canonical characters, normalized Gauss coefficient, exact
cusp/stratum reflection identities, trace convention, Mellin kernel, and
Mobius cube inversion come from OpenAI's mathematics source, pinned at
fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb. The added files assemble explicit
simultaneous divisor costs and carry them to the actual mixed source.
The assembly was developed with AI assistance. No exclusive author or
priority claim is made.

Adjacent Patterson angular estimates in the same upstream release
already supply comparable quarter-power conductor behavior in a cubic
level specialization. This companion therefore does not claim a new
analytic discovery. It makes the exact mixed local product and its
formal source connection explicit. The bound is not asserted optimal. Section 8 gives an exact extraction
which lowers the asymptotic cost of separate exponent-zero masks.

This theorem is about smooth Gauss-weighted projections. It is not an
unsmoothed Mobius-sum bound, an arbitrary marking theorem, a canonical
detector energy saving, or a zeta/Hecke zero-free improvement. In
particular, no cancellation of the cube weight's nonzero angular type
is inferred from a finite-order Hecke-character theorem.

Lean compiled the actual-source theorem, the explicit prime-product
corollary, and the separate-mask corollaries with only propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. The source
bundle records exact SHA-256 hashes and checks its experimental import
closure. Ordinary compilation is not a second-kernel check, a separate
fresh exported-proof replay, or external peer review. Read the neutral
verification summary for the checks actually completed on this bundle.
